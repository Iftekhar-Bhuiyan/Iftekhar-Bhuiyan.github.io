Executive Summary

Hospital emergency departments operate at the intersection of clinical urgency and operational capacity. When flow breaks down, patients wait longer, staff burn out, and outcomes deteriorate. This analysis examines a full year of ED admission data to answer three core questions: why are wait times rising, who is most at risk of readmission, and where should management act first?

⚠ Critical Finding

The average ED wait time was 62.8 minutes — more than double the 30-minute industry benchmark. Triage Category 3 (Urgent) patients waited an average of 56 minutes, 87% over the 30-minute target.

The Challenge

The hospital's executive team identified a year-on-year increase in patient complaints related to waiting times and an unexpectedly high 30-day readmission rate flagged during a routine clinical audit. The operations manager commissioned a data-led review with three deliverables:

  • A root-cause analysis of ED wait time variance across triage categories, shifts, and time periods
  • Identification of patient cohorts at highest risk of 30-day readmission
  • An interactive Power BI dashboard for weekly monitoring by department heads
Business Context

Every 1-minute reduction in average ED wait time is estimated to reduce "Left Without Being Seen" incidents by approximately 2.3%, directly improving both patient safety and hospital revenue.

Methodology

1
Data Acquisition & Scope Definition
Extracted 12 months of ED admission records (Jan–Dec 2024) from the hospital's patient management system. Defined 19 fields across patient demographics, triage classification, timing, department, and outcomes.
2
Data Cleaning & Validation (Python + SQL)
Used Pandas to audit missing values, detect outliers in wait times and length-of-stay, and validate triage category distributions against clinical guidelines. Standardised timestamp formats and created derived fields: hour_of_day, age_group, demand_period.
3
Exploratory Analysis (SQL — 10 Queries)
Wrote 10 targeted SQL queries covering KPI aggregation, triage benchmarking, shift-level performance, time-of-day heatmaps, complaint-level severity, and a high-risk patient flag view. Full query file included in the GitHub repository.
4
Dashboard Design & Build (Power BI)
Imported the cleaned CSV into Power BI Desktop. Built a 3-page interactive report: (1) Executive Overview, (2) Operational Deep Dive, (3) Patient Risk & Readmissions. Applied custom colour theme matching hospital branding, with cross-filtering enabled across all visuals.
5
Insight Synthesis & Recommendations
Translated analytical findings into five prioritised operational recommendations, framed in business language for non-technical stakeholders. Presented to the operations team with a structured narrative: problem → evidence → action → expected outcome.

Key Findings

01

Wait Times Exceed Benchmarks Across All Triage Levels

Not a single triage category met its national benchmark. Category 1 (Immediate) patients averaged 20.8 minutes — against a benchmark of zero wait. Category 3 (Urgent) averaged 56.2 minutes vs. the 30-minute target. The non-urgent Category 5 reached 96 minutes, driving the majority of "Left Without Being Seen" departures.

02

Night Shift is the Most Strained — 6.9% Longer Waits

Night shift (22:00–06:00) recorded the highest average wait of 65.2 minutes, compared to 61.0 for Morning. While the absolute gap is small, night shift also accounts for a disproportionate 34% of all LWBS incidents despite seeing fewer patients overall — suggesting understaffing or slower processing speed at night.

Night LWBS: 34% of all incidents
03

Patients Over 65 Have a 19.7% Readmission Rate — 2.6× the Average

The 65+ cohort drives readmissions at nearly three times the rate of the 18–34 group (7.7%). This is a known clinical challenge, but the data makes the magnitude undeniable: older patients are being discharged without adequate follow-up pathways. A targeted post-discharge care programme could significantly move this number.

Wednesday and Monday Are the Busiest Days — Demand Is Predictable

Wednesday recorded the highest patient volume (300 admissions, 15% of annual total), followed closely by Monday (297) and Friday (296). Sunday was the quietest at 267. Peak hours are 14:00–15:00 and 10:00–11:00 — both falling during shift crossover periods, pointing to a handover efficiency problem.

05

4.5% of Patients Left Without Being Seen — an Avoidable Safety Risk

89 patients departed without treatment in FY2024. Alarmingly, 12 of these were Triage Category 3 (Urgent) — meaning clinically urgent patients gave up waiting. These patients may have deteriorated at home without follow-up. This is both a patient safety risk and a reputational and legal liability for the hospital.

Power BI Setup Guide

Open Power BI Desktop → Get Data → Text/CSV and import hospital_ed_data.csv. All calculated measures (Readmission %, LWBS Rate, Wait vs Benchmark) are pre-written in DAX inside the .pbix file on GitHub. Visuals include: KPI cards, clustered bar, line trend, matrix heat table, donut, and a conditional-format table for high-risk patients.

Recommendations

Five evidence-based interventions, ranked by estimated impact and implementation effort.

HIGH PRIORITY
Introduce Nurse-Led Fast-Track for Triage 4–5
Redirect non-urgent patients to a dedicated fast-track lane staffed by triage nurses. Evidence from comparable EDs shows a 25–35% reduction in wait times for low-acuity patients within 90 days.
Est. wait reduction: −18 min for T4–5
HIGH PRIORITY
Surge Staffing on Wednesdays & Monday Mornings
Reallocate one additional nurse and one junior doctor to Wednesday morning and afternoon shifts — the consistently highest-volume window of the week, contributing 15% of annual admissions.
Est. LWBS reduction: −30%
HIGH PRIORITY
Launch 65+ Post-Discharge Follow-Up Programme
Implement a 48-hour nurse phone follow-up for all patients aged 65+ discharged from ED. The 19.7% readmission rate in this group is the single highest-leverage metric in the dataset.
Est. readmission reduction: −5–7 pp
MEDIUM PRIORITY
Investigate Night Shift Handover Efficiency
Night shift has the highest average wait (65.2 min) and an outsized share of LWBS incidents. A structured handover protocol audit and staggered end-of-shift overlap are recommended.
Target: align night wait with day shift
ONGOING
Embed Power BI Dashboard in Weekly Ops Review
Publish the dashboard to Power BI Service and embed the KPI page into the existing Monday morning operations meeting. Set automated alerts for when wait time or LWBS rate exceeds defined thresholds.
Enables real-time operational response

Lessons Learned

The most valuable insight from this project had nothing to do with the numbers — it was about framing. A 14.4% readmission rate is a statistic. "We discharged 288 patients last year who returned within 30 days — 107 of whom were over 65 and could have been helped with a phone call" is a decision. The analyst's job is to close the gap between the two.

  • Dirty timestamps are everywhere. About 3% of admission records had formatting inconsistencies between 12-hour and 24-hour time — caught by a simple Pandas value_counts audit.
  • Business benchmarks must be defined before analysis, not after. Agreeing on the 30-minute T3 target upfront made the findings unambiguous.
  • Dashboard consumers ≠ data consumers. The final Power BI report removed most of the granular SQL-level detail and replaced it with plain-language KPI labels and conditional formatting — what clinicians actually use.
